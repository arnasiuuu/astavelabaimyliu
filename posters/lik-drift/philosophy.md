# Smoke Ledger

**Philosophy:** Motion recorded as evidence. The fleeting thing — a slide, a plume, a second of commitment — is catalogued with the calm of a field archivist.

**Space and form.** The composition is built on a strict, quiet grid: one dominant frame that carries the event, and a disciplined row of smaller frames that act as its supporting record. Margins are generous and exact. Nothing floats; every edge answers to another edge. The large image is the testimony, the small frames are the footnotes, and the empty dark field between them is the silence that lets both be heard. The layout must look meticulously crafted, as if every alignment was argued over for hours.

**Color and material.** A near-black, slightly warm ground, like exposed film base, carries a fine photographic grain across the whole surface so image and paper feel cut from the same material. Color is rationed. The photographs supply the only warmth — autumn gold, haze, asphalt grey — and a single chromatic accent is allowed: three narrow bands of saturated color, placed once, with the precision of a calibration strip. Everything else is bone white and graphite. The restraint itself is the product of painstaking chromatic judgment.

**Scale and rhythm.** One gesture of monumental type, condensed and heavy, set to the full measure of the grid so it reads as architecture rather than as a headline. Against it, tiny monospaced labels — frame numbers, indices, short phrases — repeat at a steady cadence, the way an archive tags its specimens. The jump between huge and tiny is the rhythm of the piece: a shout, then a ledger of whispers.

**Composition and balance.** Weight sits high in the large frame and settles low in the typography and the contact row, so the eye travels down the sheet like smoke drifting downwind. Hairline rules divide zones without decorating them. Text never sits on top of imagery; it lives in its own clear space with ample breathing room. Balance comes from proportion, not symmetry.

**Visual hierarchy.** Image first, mark second, record third. The poster should feel like the cover plate of a meticulously assembled archive — master-level execution, the product of deep expertise, where every millimetre was considered and nothing was left to chance.
